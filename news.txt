Hariku 2.0: The Wait is Over. A Year in the Making.
Posted by Raf ♾ on June 18, 2026

Hello everyone,
Raf here.

Take a deep breath. I know I just did. 

It feels almost surreal to be typing these words. If we rewind the clock to June 1, 2025, over an entire year ago, we launched the very first beta of Hariku V1. Back then, it was just an ambitious idea, a monolithic application packed to the brim with features trying to do everything at once. We dreamed big, we built fast, and together, we created something truly special.

But to understand why today is so monumental, we have to look back at the chaotic, beautiful, and sometimes painful road that led us here. 

The Tipping Point
Many of you remember September 2025. We had just released the 1.6 updates, introducing the AI Mood Summary, the Period Tracker, and surviving the "evil Windows Defender" battles. I had laid out a grand roadmap for version 1.7.0. But behind the scenes, I was drowning. After countless hours of tinkering, deep frustration, and moments of severe burnout, I hit a massive concrete wall. The codebase had become too heavy, too intertwined. Fixing one bug in the calendar would somehow break the period tracker. 

That was the day I made the terrifying decision to sunset version 1.7.0. I realized a fundamental truth: to build the future we envisioned, we couldn't just add another floor; we had to tear down the house and rebuild the foundation. 

The Long Silence & The Distractions
I promised you Hariku 2.0. I promised a modular, lightweight future. But I didn't realize it would take nearly a year of absolute obsession. There were detours, of course. Some of you might remember my month-long disappearance into the rabbit hole of building a text-based MUD game, or the terrifying weeks where severe swelling in my ear and neck forced me away from the keyboard entirely. 

But through the hospital visits, the distractions, and the late-night coding marathons, the vision for Hariku 2.0 never faded. It only grew sharper.

A Blank Canvas: The Extension-Driven Revolution
Today, the silence is broken. Hariku 2.0 is alive. 

When you first launch it, you might notice something terrifying: it feels empty. Do not panic. This is by design. The core principle of Hariku 2.0 is extreme minimalism. The core application handles only the absolute essentials. 

Everything else has been completely decoupled and transformed into standalone "Extensions." We built a proprietary Extension Manager powered by a highly robust Event Bus system. This means if the new "My Diary" extension crashes, the core application will just shrug it off and keep running perfectly. No more fatal boot loops. No more silent failures. You now have the ultimate power to tailor Hariku exactly to your workflow. This is the NVDA-inspired plugin architecture I dreamed of last year, finally made real.

Built for Resilience: The New Safe Mode
Because we are now relying on an ecosystem of extensions, I had to ensure you were never locked out of your own application. That is why Hariku 2.0 introduces a true, hardware-level Safe Mode. If an extension ever goes rogue, simply hold the 'Shift' key while launching Hariku. At the exact millisecond of detection, you will hear a subtle, deeply satisfying "click" (resembling a soft camera shutter). That sound is Hariku telling you: "I've got you." The Extension Manager will be completely bypassed, launching only the naked core.

The Early Testing Phase Begins Now
I want to be completely transparent: today marks the beginning of our Early Testing Phase. While the core architecture is rock-solid, many of the extensions that replace V1's functionality are still actively being rewritten and ported over. You will encounter bugs. You will notice missing pieces. 

But when I fix a bug now, you won't need to download a massive Hariku core update. You will simply update the extension via the built-in Hariku Store.

Welcome to the new era. The doors are wide open. Any developer can now write an extension and share it with the world. The future of Hariku is no longer dictated just by me; it is dictated by what we can build together.

Thank you for waiting. Let's make some magic.
