2. EXTENSIONS
================================================================================

Extensions add features to Hariku. They are Python code and are not
sandboxed: an extension can do whatever Hariku can do on your computer,
including reading your files and keystrokes and using the network. That is
why Hariku checks them.

THE STORE
  The Hariku store's catalogue is on GitHub Pages, and its extensions are
  downloaded only over HTTPS from Hariku's own GitHub releases. Hariku
  refuses a download from any other address.

THE TRUST LIST AND YOUR CONSENT
  - Before Hariku loads an extension package (.hrk) that is not one of its
    official extensions, it works out the package's SHA-256 fingerprint and
    looks for it in the list of trusted fingerprints, which is published
    with the store. Extensions accepted into the store are on that list.
  - A package that is not on the list, for example because it came from
    somewhere else, or because the list could not be downloaded, is not
    loaded until you agree. Hariku warns you that the extension will have
    full access to your computer and asks you. If you say no, it is skipped.
    Hariku asks again the next time it starts.
  - Extension folders that you put in place yourself, for example while you
    write an extension, are loaded without these checks.
  - Safe Mode: hold Shift while Hariku starts, and it loads no extensions at
    all. In the Extension Manager you can also disable any extension without
    removing it.

THIRD-PARTY EXTENSIONS
  Extensions by other people are their authors' responsibility, including
  what they do with your data and the license they choose. Install them only
  from people you trust.

WRITING YOUR OWN
  The Hariku Developer SDK has the guide, a template, examples and the
  packager: https://github.com/InfiArtt/hariku-sdk
  Its template and examples are under the MIT License, so you can build on
  them under any license. To offer an extension in the store, use the store
  submission form (see Contact).

3. PRIVACY
================================================================================

Hariku collects no analytics or telemetry. It sends no usage statistics, and
there is nothing to opt out of.

YOUR DATA STAYS ON YOUR COMPUTER
  Your calendar, reminders, routines, profile, places, settings and extension
  data are kept on your computer, in %APPDATA%\Hariku2. Hariku's developer
  can't see them. What you type for a quick reminder or to Aruna, the command
  bar, is understood on your computer by rules built into Hariku, with no AI
  and no online service, and it is not stored. The only exception is the
  Ask Hariku extension, and only if you turn it on (see below). Hariku Voice
  with the Windows voices also speaks on your computer.

WHAT HARIKU CONNECTS TO BY ITSELF
  When Hariku starts, it downloads a few small files from its GitHub Pages
  site (infiartt.github.io):
    - version.json, to see whether a newer Hariku exists;
    - the store catalogue, to see whether your extensions have updates (to
      stop this, choose "Do nothing" for extension updates in Preferences,
      Extensions);
    - the list of trusted extension fingerprints.
  These requests carry only what every web request carries, such as your IP
  address and a Hariku user agent: no identifier, no account details and
  none of your data. GitHub's privacy statement covers them.

WHEN YOU ASK FOR IT
  - Opening the store (or the welcome's page of suggested extensions), or
    installing or updating an extension or Hariku itself, downloads from
    Hariku's GitHub Pages site and GitHub releases.
  - Searching for an address in Preferences, Places sends what you typed and
    your Hariku language to OpenStreetMap's Nominatim
    (nominatim.openstreetmap.org), only when you press Enter or Search
    address.
  - Searching for a city (in Places, in the welcome, or in an extension's
    settings) sends what you typed and your Hariku language to Open-Meteo
    (geocoding-api.open-meteo.com).
  - A short Google Maps link that you paste is sent to Google once, when you
    press Use, to find the place it points to.
  - During the welcome, your city's weather comes from Open-Meteo
    (api.open-meteo.com), which receives the city's position rounded to
    about 1 kilometre.
  - Menu items such as Community & Support and Report an Issue, and the
    "Open URL" routine action, open pages in your web browser.

CRASH REPORTS
  When Hariku crashes, it asks whether to send a crash report, and sends it
  only if you agree. In the same dialog you can choose to be asked every
  time (the default), to always send reports, or to never send them. A
  report goes to infiartt.com, the server of Hariku's developer, and contains
  the Hariku version, your Windows version, Hariku's language and the error
  details. The error details include a traceback, which can contain file
  paths from your computer. Either way, the error is written to Hariku's log
  file in your Temp folder, which stays on your computer.

EXTENSIONS AND THE INTERNET
  The official extensions connect only for their own features, and only
  while they are installed and enabled; several never go online. Some check
  in the background while Hariku runs, for example Weather (about every 30
  minutes, once it has a place) and Earthquakes & Tsunami (about once a
  minute while its alerts are on). A few examples:
    - Weather, Sea Conditions and Air Quality get their data from Open-Meteo
      for your place rounded to about 1 kilometre, never your exact
      location.
    - Earthquakes & Tsunami downloads the same public files for everyone
      from BMKG (and from the USGS, if you turn on worldwide earthquakes);
      your location stays on your computer.
    - Google Calendar Reader downloads holiday data from GitHub Pages and
      the calendar links you add.
    - Edge Voices sends the text being read to Microsoft's speech service,
      only if you choose Edge voices.
    - Piper Voices and Voice Control download their programs, voices and
      speech models from GitHub and Hugging Face only when you work with
      them in Preferences; speaking and speech recognition then run on your
      computer.
    - Orbit, a multiplayer game, connects to its game server (by default at
      infiartt.com) when you play, or when Hariku starts if you turn that
      on.
    - Dropbox talks to Dropbox only after you connect your account, and
      Hariku Account Manager talks to infiartt.com only when you sign in.
    - Ask Hariku sends nothing until you turn on one of its two features in
      Preferences, Ask Hariku. Then a how-to question you ask Aruna, or a
      sentence Aruna didn't understand (with the names of Hariku's own
      commands), goes to Hariku's AI service on Cloudflare Workers. Only
      the words and their language are sent, never your reminders, diary,
      finances, places or name, and the service keeps no logs. Aruna asks
      you before doing anything the AI suggests.
    - Clipboard History, Timer & Alarm and Sleep Pattern send nothing.
  Third-party extensions can make their own connections, which their
  authors are responsible for.

THE FULL LIST
  This is a summary. The full, exact list is in PRIVACY.md: every connection
  that Hariku and each official extension makes, what it sends, and what is
  stored. PRIVACY.md comes with Hariku and is also online at
  https://github.com/InfiArtt/hariku-core/blob/main/PRIVACY.md

4. ACCESSIBILITY COMMITMENT
================================================================================

Hariku is made first of all for people who use a screen reader. Its developer
commits to the following:

  - Every part of Hariku's own interface will be properly labelled and can be
    used with the keyboard and a screen reader.
  - Every control will have a meaningful name that NVDA, JAWS and other
    screen readers read correctly.
  - No core feature will be out of reach for someone who uses only the
    keyboard and screen reader output.
  - An accessibility regression in Hariku's core is a high-priority bug and
    will be fixed promptly.
  - Extension authors are strongly encouraged, though not required, to meet
    the same standard. The store may decline to feature an extension that
    is not reasonably accessible.

5. UPDATES
================================================================================

HARIKU ITSELF
  A few seconds after it starts, Hariku reads version.json from its GitHub
  Pages site to see whether a newer version exists. You can also check at
  any time with Help, Check for updates, or with the Check for Updates
  button in Preferences, General. A copy run from the source code checks
  only when you ask.

  When there is a newer version, Hariku shows what's new and downloads
  nothing until you choose "Download & Install Now". Updates are never
  installed without that choice.
    - An optional update can be skipped.
    - A recommended update can be put off three times while Hariku runs;
      after that it is shown as critical.
    - A critical update is meant for serious bugs and security problems.
      When it is marked as required, or your version is older than the
      oldest one still supported, its dialog has no button to put it off
      and stays open until you update. This is meant only for real
      emergencies.

HOW AN UPDATE IS CHECKED
  - The installer must come over HTTPS from Hariku's own GitHub releases or
    its GitHub Pages site. Any other address is refused.
  - Its SHA-256 fingerprint must match the one in version.json. If it
    doesn't, or version.json gives none, the installer is deleted and never
    run.
  - Its Windows code signature (Authenticode) is checked too, and an
    installer with an invalid signature is refused. Hariku's releases are
    not code-signed yet, so for now an unsigned installer is accepted when
    its fingerprint matches.
  Then Hariku runs the installer silently and closes, and the installer
  replaces Hariku's program files. Your data in %APPDATA%\Hariku2 is not
  touched.

EXTENSIONS
  A few seconds after it starts, Hariku compares your store extensions with
  the store's catalogue. In Preferences, Extensions you choose what happens:
  Hariku tells you about updates (the default), updates them automatically,
  or does nothing, and then it doesn't check at all. Updated extensions come
  from Hariku's GitHub releases, are checked like any other package when
  they are loaded, and an automatic update takes effect when Hariku
  restarts.
