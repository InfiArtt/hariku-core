# Timer & Alarm

Set alarms and timers by typing or saying them to Aruna, in English or Indonesian. When
one is due it rings with a Windows alarm sound and says what it is, even while Hariku is
in the tray. It needs Hariku 2.9 or newer. Everything stays on your computer: nothing is
sent over the internet.

## Getting started

Install Timer & Alarm from the Extension Store. Then open Aruna, Hariku's command bar,
with Ctrl+Alt+Backspace, type what you want, for example "alarm tomorrow at 6 AM" or
"timer 10 minutes", and press Enter. With the Voice Control extension you can say it
instead.

Your alarms and timers, and the sounds, are on the Timer & Alarm page in Preferences.

## Setting an alarm

Tell Aruna the alarm with its time, and a name if you like:

- "alarm tomorrow at 6 AM"
- "set alarm tomorrow at 2 for football"
- "wake me up at 5"
- "alarm in 10 minutes"
- "alarm besok jam 5 pagi"
- "bangunkan aku jam 4.30"

Other ways to start work too, such as "set an alarm", "add an alarm", "new alarm", "pasang
alarm" or "setel alarm". The words that aren't the date or the time become the alarm's
name, like "football" above.

Aruna reads the alarm back before setting it, with the day, the date and the time with AM
or PM, for example: Alarm "football", tomorrow, Saturday 26 September, at 2:00 AM. Set it?

- To set it, press Enter, or say "yes" or "set it". Aruna says how long it is until then.
- To leave it, say "no" or press Escape.

A time you give without a day that has already passed today, such as "alarm at 9 AM" at 10
in the morning, is set for tomorrow, and the read-back says so.

### Correcting the time

If the read-back has the wrong time, say just the right time, for example "at 2 PM" or
"jam 2 siang". You can say "no" first. This works for two minutes after the read-back:
Aruna keeps the alarm's day and name, puts in the new time and asks again.

### How an hour alone is read

When you say an hour from 1 to 12 without AM, PM or a part of the day, Aruna has to
choose:

- With a day ("tomorrow at 2") or a repeat ("every day at 5"): the first such hour of that
  day, so 2:00 AM or 5:00 AM. If that day is today and the morning one has passed, the
  afternoon one. 12 is noon.
- With no day ("at 2"): the next 2 o'clock to come, morning or afternoon.
- "Wake me up" and "bangunkan aku" always mean the morning.

The read-back always says AM or PM, so a wrong guess is easy to hear and correct. A time
with AM or PM, with a part of the day ("jam 7 malam"), or in 24-hour form ("19:00") is
taken as you said it.

### Repeating alarms

Put the repeat in the sentence: "alarm every day at 5 AM for the morning walk", "alarm
every Monday at 7", "alarm setiap hari jam 5 sholat subuh". An alarm can repeat every day
or every few days, on a weekday every week or every few weeks, every month or every year.
It can't ring more often than once a day, so "every hour" isn't possible.

### When Aruna can't set it

- No time, as in "alarm tomorrow" or "alarm besok pagi": Aruna asks for the time instead
  of guessing. Say the alarm again with its time.
- A date or a time that doesn't exist, or one that has already passed: Aruna tells you.
- Two dates or times in one sentence: Aruna uses the first and says so in the read-back.
- An alarm you already have: Aruna says so and doesn't add it twice.

## Timers

A timer starts at once, without a question:

- "timer 10 minutes"
- "timer tea 3 minutes"
- "set a timer for an hour and a half"
- "timer setengah jam"
- "hitung mundur 90 detik"

Start with "timer" or with words like "set a timer", "start a timer", "countdown", "pasang
timer" or "hitung mundur". A name goes after the word "timer" ("timer tea 3 minutes"). You
can give the length in hours, minutes and seconds, in digits or in words ("twenty-five
minutes", "satu setengah jam"). A timer runs for 24 hours at most; for longer, set an
alarm. Several timers can run at the same time.

## Checking, changing and cancelling

Ask Aruna:

- "list my alarms" or "daftar alarm": what is set and what is running.
- "how long is left", "how long is left on the tea timer", "sisa timer mie".
- "cancel the tea timer", "batalkan timer mie": cancels it at once.
- "cancel all timers" or "batalkan semua timer".
- "delete the football alarm", "hapus alarm nonton bola": Aruna asks first.
- "delete all alarms" or "hapus semua alarm": Aruna asks first.

An alarm without a name can be picked by its time ("delete the 6 AM alarm", "hapus alarm
jam 6"), and a timer without a name by its length ("cancel the 10 minute timer"). When
more than one fits, or you say no name and you have several, Aruna reads them out so you
can say which.

To change an alarm, delete it and set it again. You can also delete alarms and timers on
the Preferences page (see Settings).

## When it rings

When an alarm or timer is due, you hear its sound and then what it is, such as "Alarm:
football." or "The timer "tea" is done.", again every 10 seconds. It rings even when
Hariku is in the tray and during quiet hours, because you set it. It opens no window and
doesn't move your focus. If you set Hariku Voice to read reminders, alarms are spoken in
that voice too.

### Stopping it

- Press any key. Moving the mouse doesn't stop it, and neither does a key you are already
  holding down as it starts. A moment later Hariku says which one it stopped.
- Or tell Aruna "stop", "stop the alarm", "I'm awake", "matikan alarm" or "aku sudah
  bangun".

### Snoozing it

Tell Aruna "snooze" or "tunda" to hear it again after your snooze length (5 minutes unless
you change it), or give a length: "snooze 10 minutes", "tunda 10 menit". This also works
up to two minutes after a key stopped the ring.

### Missed alarms

If nobody stops it, it rings for 3 minutes (you can change this) and then counts as
missed. The next time you press a key, Hariku tells you what you missed. An alarm that
came due while Hariku was closed, or while the computer was asleep, is mentioned once when
you are back; if Hariku starts within the ring length, it still rings.

If you use the Morning Briefing extension, the briefing tells you the alarms still to come
that day.

## Settings

Open Preferences (Ctrl+P in Hariku's window) and choose Timer & Alarm.

- Alarms and timers: a list with Name, When or time left, and Repeats. The Delete key or
  the Remove button deletes the selected one, after asking.
- Stop ringing: stops what is ringing now.
- Alarm sound and Timer sound: Windows' own alarm and ring sounds, or Hariku's alarm and
  timer tones. Test the alarm sound and Test the timer sound play three seconds of your
  choice.
- Ring for (then it counts as missed): from 1 to 15 minutes, 3 by default.
- Snooze for: from 1 to 30 minutes, 5 by default.

Remove and Stop ringing act at once. The sounds and lengths are saved when you press OK or
Apply.

## Keys and commands

These actions have no key by default, because any key already stops a ring. You can give
each one a key in Preferences, Input Gestures, where they are under "Timer and Alarm";
tick "Make this shortcut Global" so the key works in every window.

- Stop the ringing alarm or timer: "stop", "stop the alarm", "I'm awake", "matikan alarm",
  "berhenti".
- Snooze the alarm: "snooze", "tunda", "tunda alarm".
- Say the time left on your timers: "how long is left", "time left", "sisa timer", "berapa
  lama lagi".
- List your alarms and timers: "list my alarms", "my timers", "daftar alarm", "alarmku".
- Cancel a timer: "cancel the timer", "batalkan timer".
- Cancel all timers: "cancel all timers", "batalkan semua timer".
- Delete an alarm: "delete the alarm", "hapus alarm". Said without a name, this lists your
  alarms so you can say which one.

Setting an alarm ("alarm ...", "wake me up ...", "bangunkan aku ..."), starting a timer
("timer ...", "hitung mundur ..."), cancelling, snoozing for a length and asking the time
left on one timer all take your own words after the command, as shown in the sections
above.
