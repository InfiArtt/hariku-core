# Orbit

Orbit is a multiplayer text game you play with other Hariku users. Its hub is a space
station orbiting the Earth, and from there you can travel to other worlds. You walk by
compass, talk with other players, work a job, farm, mine, trade, play games and raise a
family, all by typing or speaking short commands. It needs Hariku 2.9 or newer.

Orbit is played in English: every command and every name in the game is English, and so
are its window and its settings. Words in another language aren't understood; the game
answers them with a hint to type "help".

## Getting started

### Your first visit

1. Open Preferences and choose the Orbit page.
2. In "Character name (for your first visit)", type a name: 3 to 20 letters and digits,
   starting with a letter. Nobody else on the station may already have it.
3. In "Job (for your first visit)", choose Pilot, Engineer, Trader, Scientist or
   Security officer. Your job decides where you work (see Work and progress).
4. Press Connect.

You arrive at the Dock with 100 credits and a compass. The server keeps your character
from then on, so the name and job boxes are only used that first time. Leave "Server
address" as it is.

### Opening the Orbit window

Run "Open Orbit", or tell Aruna "orbit", "open orbit" or "play orbit". "Open Orbit" has
no key by default; give it one in Preferences, Input Gestures. Opening the
window connects you if you aren't connected yet, and puts you in the Command field.

### The window

- Messages: every line of the game, newest last. It is read-only text: read it with the
  arrow keys like any text. A reply of several parts comes a part to a line: a room's
  name, its description, its exits, who is here and the things to look at are separate
  lines, and so are the entries of a list (who is online, your things, the prices), so
  Up and Down Arrow read them one by one. A new line never moves your focus, and while
  you read in the box, your place stays where it is.
- Command: type a command and press Enter. Up and Down Arrow bring back your earlier
  commands.
- Connect, which becomes Leave Orbit while you're connected.
- Help: Orbit's own help.
- Remind me of the next event: a Hariku reminder before the next event (see Events).
- Settings: Orbit's page in Preferences.
- Status: whether you're connected, as whom and with which job.

The window's title also says whether you're connected, for example "Orbit: Connected".

### Help in the game

Type "help" for the list of help topics, in four groups:

- The station: "help moving", "help talking", "help people".
- Making a living: "help work", "help money", "help shops", "help ships".
- Fun: "help casino", "help arcade", "help events", "help hunt", "help crews",
  "help duels", "help progress".
- Life: "help pets", "help family", "help weddings".

"help settings" lists the settings you can type. "repeat" (or "again") reads the last
message again, and "status" says who you're connected as, where you are and how many are online.

"x here" says what you can do in the room you're in (see What can I do here?).

## Moving around

### Walking by compass

You walk one room at a time. Type a direction: n, s, e, w, ne, nw, se, sw, u (up) and
d (down), or the whole word: north, south, east, west, northeast, northwest, southeast,
southwest, up and down. "u" is always up.

If there's no way in the direction you typed, Orbit says so and names the exits.

### Looking around

- "look" (or "l") describes the room a part to a line: its name, its description, its
  exits ("Exits: north, southwest, down."), who is here, the residents, and the things to
  look at. It's read aloud all at once; in the Messages box each part is its own line.
- "look north" peeks into the next room without walking there; "peer north" says a
  little more (see The room and its people), and "exits" names where each way leads.
- "look at" and a thing or a person describes it, for example "look at earth" at the
  Observation Deck, where you see the real Earth below as it is right now.

### What can I do here?

"x here" lists the commands that work in the room you're in, one a line, each with a
short hint, and ends with "Type help for everything else." In the Cantina:

- "Here in the Cantina you can:"
- "list: what the Cantina bar sells, and the prices"
- "buy iced coffee: buy from the Cantina bar"
- "talk to Rocco: hear what Rocco can tell you about"
- "ask Rocco about gossip: one of the things Rocco talks about"
- "look at bar: the things here are bar, jukebox and menu"
- "x Rocco: what you can do with someone or something here"

It lists only what works where you stand: a market's prices, a shop's list, the farm,
mining and salvage, the casino's games, the arcade, the temple, the family and wedding
desks, duels in the contest zones, the Wombat, the ferry, the Gate, your ship, your
job's work at your workplace, your mission, your cabin, an event on here, and the
residents present. What works anywhere (look, inventory, who, say...) is left to help.
"what can I do here", "examine here", "commands here" and "help here" say the same. In
the dark you only hear that it's too dark: wear a headlamp.

"x" and a name says what you can do with someone or something: "x Rocco" (talk, ask
about his topics, greet him, a gift he'd like, his bar's list), "x Maya" (whisper, give,
a gesture, a trade, her profile, a duel in a contest zone, a coin flip at the casino...),
"x headlamp" (wear it, or take it off), "x iced coffee" (drink it), "x coffee" at a
market (buy or sell it here), "x jukebox" or "x me". "look at" still describes.

The station has three decks: the Upper Deck (command and science), the Main Deck (the
Promenade, the shops, the Cantina and the crew cabins) and the Lower Deck (Engineering,
the Cargo Bay and the Dock). The Mall Ring is a lift ride up from the Upper Deck, and the
hull outside and the Asteroid Belt have rooms of their own.

### Locked doors, dark tunnels and space

- Some doors are locked, and a keycard opens them. Everyone gets a crew keycard at
  level 2 and an officer keycard at level 10; engineers also get a technician keycard at
  level 3. Gearworks on the Mall Ring sells them too.
- The maintenance tunnels and the Crystal Cave are dark. Without a headlamp you only feel
  the way you came in; wear one ("wear headlamp") and you see the room and its exits.
  Star Supply and Gearworks sell headlamps.
- Outside the hull there's no air. You need an EVA suit (from Gearworks, from level 2):
  "wear EVA suit", then go out through the Airlock, south of the Dock. The suit holds 3
  minutes of air, and an oxygen tank adds 3 more. Your suit beeps a warning at 60 and at
  20 seconds. If the air runs out, the station's tow drone brings you back to the Airlock
  for 20 credits.

### The Wombat shuttle

At the Dock, "ride the Wombat" flies the little mining shuttle to the Asteroid Belt, where
you can mine. The fare is 5 credits from the Dock (pilots ride free), and the ride back from
the Belt Platform is free.

## Finding your way

- "way to the cantina" gives the steps from where you are, with the same direction in a
  row counted once. From the Dock: "To the Cantina: 2 east, south, up, north, then 2
  west." A long way also says how many steps it is.
- The way also guides you there. After each step you take, one short line says what
  comes next ("Then 2 west."). If you step off the route, it finds the way again from
  where you are ("Off the route. From here: south."), and when you get there it says
  "You've arrived at the Cantina.", with a short sound.
- "guide me to the cantina" does the same. "guide" alone repeats the rest of the way, and
  "stop guide" ends it. Logging out, the Gate, the ferry and boarding a ship end it too.
- "way to the market" leads you to the nearest market.
- "go to the cantina" walks there when it's the next room; when it's further, it tells
  you the way and guides you. There's no teleporting: you walk each step.
- "map" describes the deck around you.
- "where am I" names the room, the deck and the exits.
- "compass" says which way you last walked and which deck you're on.

The way only goes through doors you can open, and one-way exits only their way. It takes
the Wombat when that's part of it. In a dark tunnel the guide still knows the next step
when you can't see it, and it tells you when the next step needs your EVA suit on.

You can always ask the way to the Dock, the Promenade, the Cantina, Star Supply, the
markets, the lift lobbies, your workplace and your cabin. For other places you need a
mapper:

- A pocket mapper (Star Supply) remembers every room you've been to, so "way to" and
  "map" know all of them.
- A holo mapper (Gearworks, from level 4) knows every public room, visited or not, and
  gives a fuller map of each deck.
- A beacon (Star Supply) marks a room: "place beacon" leaves it there, and "way to
  beacon" leads you back from anywhere.
- A crew scanner (Gearworks): "scan" says who is in the rooms around you.
- A communicator (Gearworks): "locate Maya" says where Maya is and the way there, and you
  hear when your friends log in. "friends" lists them; "add friend Maya" and "remove
  friend Maya" change the list.

"way to Karmina", or the name of any other world, tells you how to travel there.

## Talking

### Saying, whispering and shouting

- "say hello everyone", or start a line with a quote mark: everyone in the room hears it.
- "whisper Maya see you on the deck": only Maya hears it, wherever she is.
- "shout hello everyone": everyone online hears it, once every 10 seconds.

### Gestures

Type a gesture alone, or with a name: "smile", "wave", "laugh", "nod", "shrug", "clap",
"cheer", "sigh", "bow", "dance", "hug Maya", "wave to Maya", and more: "kiss Maya", "wink",
"high five Sam" (see The room and its people). Each one has its own sound.

### Who is here

- "who" lists the players online.
- "look at Maya" describes her: her rank, what she wears and the description she wrote.
- "profile Maya" shows her progress; "profile" alone shows yours.
- "describe" and a short line sets your own description, the one others hear when they
  look at you; "describe" alone reads it back.
- "inventory" (or "i") says your credits and what's in your bag.

### Your cabin and visitors

Your cabin is south of the Crew Quarters on the Main Deck. It's private: "invite Maya"
lets Maya in for 10 minutes, and she types "visit" and your name from the Crew Quarters.
"uninvite Maya" takes it back. Furniture you buy is set out in your cabin by itself.

## The room and its people

Every command here belongs to a place or a moment: where it doesn't work, Orbit says so
briefly, and where it does ("The jukebox is in the Cantina."). "x here" always lists what
works in the room you're in.

### Sitting, lying down and sleeping

- "sit" sits you down on the room's furniture if it has some (a bar stool in the Cantina,
  a bench, a window seat, the lawn in the Sky Park, your bunk or sofa in your cabin), or
  on the floor. "sit on the booth", "sit on a bench" and "sit on the floor" choose.
- "lie down", "lie on the lawn"; "sleep" lies you down and you fall asleep.
- "stand" (or "stand up", "get up") gets you up. At the casino's card table, while you're
  playing a hand, "stand" is blackjack's; anywhere else it's getting up.
- Everyone in the room hears it ("Maya sits down on a bar stool."), and a look says it
  ("Maya is sitting on a bar stool."). "who" says who is asleep.
- Walking stands you up by itself: "You stand up and walk east to the West Promenade."
- Asleep, doing something wakes you first ("You wake up."); looking and asking don't.
  "wake" wakes you and gets you up; "wake Maya" wakes a sleeping friend gently.
- A seat holds so many people: "Every bar stool is taken."

### Following and leading

- "follow Maya" asks Maya if you may follow her; "lead Maya" offers to lead her. She
  answers "accept" or "decline".
- Then you walk a step behind her: "You follow Maya north to the Promenade." Both rooms
  hear it: "Sam follows Maya north.", "Sam arrives, following Maya."
- "stop following", "stop leading" or "disband" ends it. So does walking off on your own,
  a door you can't open, a private room, falling asleep, or the leader leaving Orbit.

### The ways out and a glimpse next door

- "exits" (or "ex") says each way out on a line of its own, with where it leads: "North:
  the Promenade", "Southwest: the Maintenance Junction (locked, dark)", "Ride the Wombat:
  to the Belt Platform". In the dark you only feel the way you came in.
- "peer north" glimpses the next room: its name, the start of its description, who is
  there and what lies about. The room sees you peer.

### Gestures, dice and the time

- Besides smile, wave, laugh, nod, shrug, clap, cheer, sigh, bow, dance and hug: kiss (on
  the cheek), wink, giggle, cry, yawn, blush, poke, high five, thank (or "thank you"),
  salute, facepalm, stretch, ponder, comfort and shake hands. Alone or with a name: "kiss
  Maya", "high five Sam", "shake hands with Rocco". Residents answer.
- "emote waves hello", or ":waves hello", says your own gesture after your name: "Rafli
  waves hello."
- "roll" rolls two dice for the room to see; "roll a die", "roll 2d6", "roll d20". The
  station's computer rolls them, fairly.
- "time" says the station's time (UTC) and, on another world, its own local time.
- "afk making tea" says you're away from the keyboard: others see it, and whoever
  whispers to you hears it. Any command brings you back.

### Things you put down

- "drop 2 coffee" (or "put down coffee") puts things on the floor; "put coffee on the
  table" where something holds things (a bar, a table, the Reading Room's swap shelf).
- "get coffee" (or "take", "pick up") picks up what lies here, anyone's; "get coffee from
  the table".
- "throw crackers to Maya": anyone in the room can "catch" it within a few seconds, or it
  lands on the floor.
- A look says what lies about: "On the floor: 2 sacks of coffee." After half an hour a
  cleaning drone gives things back to whoever put them down. Goods on the floor still
  count in your bag, and what you wear, devices and keycards can't be put down.
- "undress" takes off your clothes and title.

### The jukebox and the pond

- In the Cantina, "jukebox" lists its songs; "jukebox 3" or "pick song moonlight" plays
  one for everyone there, for 2 credits.
- In Willow Nook, north of the Sky Park, "fish" casts a line. When it tugs, "reel", quick!
  Perch, carp and trout go in your bag, and the Spice Market buys them; a golden koi is let
  go, for luck. Up to 30 fish a day.

### Again

"again" (or "!") does your last command again. If you mistype a command, Orbit asks "Did
you mean exits?".

## Reading and listening

Everything that happens is read aloud as it comes, a whole reply at once, and also goes
to the Messages box, a part to a line.

### Who reads: the "Read with" setting

"Read with" in Preferences, Orbit, chooses who reads the game. You can also type it in
the game.

- "Mixed: talk and announcements in Hariku Voice, the rest by NVDA" (the default).
  What people say, whisper and shout, crew chat and the station's announcements are
  spoken in Hariku Voice; everything else (rooms, exits, gestures, what your commands
  do) your screen reader reads at once, at its own speed. Typed: "reader mixed".
- "NVDA only (fastest)": your screen reader reads everything, whole lines, other
  players' words included. Typed: "reader nvda", "nvda only" or "screen reader".
- "Hariku Voice for everything": all of it in Hariku Voice. Typed: "reader voices" or
  "all voices".

Commands you give through Aruna are always answered aloud.

### Players' voices

When Hariku Voice reads a player's words, each player can have a voice of their own. That
needs "A different voice for each player (Hariku Voice)" on, and at least two of Hariku
Voice's English voices: players' voices are picked among those. Edge Voices or Piper
Voices, from the Extension Store, add more.

- "my voice 3" chooses the voice others hear for you, from 1 to 10, and plays a sample.
  The same number is always the same voice on one computer, but each computer uses its
  own voices for it.
- "my voice auto" goes back to a voice picked from your name. "my voice" alone says
  which one you have.
- A player who hasn't chosen gets a voice picked from their name. The residents have
  voices of their own too.

With fewer than two English voices, Orbit tells you so once, and everyone is read in
your usual voice.

### Names before words

When Hariku Voice reads a player's line, the name comes first, briefly, in the usual
voice ("Sam:", "Sam, whispering:"), and then the words in Sam's voice. Your own words
are spoken in your own voice too; a whisper first says who it went to ("To Maya:").
"names off" leaves the names out; "my lines off" only confirms your own lines ("Sent.").
Your words become speech only on each computer that hears them: Orbit sends the text you
typed, never your voice.

### The Messages box

The Messages box keeps the last 500 lines or so, always whole, even when only a short
confirmation was read aloud. A reply of several parts (a room, your things, who is
online, the prices, the events, help) takes a line for each part. "repeat" reads the
newest reply again, whole. What you choose not to hear (see Settings) still goes to the
box, except the words of players you ignore.

### Sounds and ambience

Sounds come from the side where things happen: your steps on metal, carpet, grass, stone,
wood or rock, towards the way you walk; someone arriving from the west on your left; the
lift humming, an airlock hissing, each gesture, coins, a harvest, the pick on rock, the
dice, cards and reels, the temple's bell, all with the room's echo. Many are recordings
from Kenney's free (CC0) sound packs.

While the window is open, a quiet ambience of the place plays. It keeps playing while
your screen reader reads the game, and dips softly while Hariku Voice speaks (the
players' voices), so the words stay clear. Both have their own switch and volume in
Settings.

## Work and progress

### Your job

Type "work" at your workplace, then answer with numbers.

- Engineers, in Engineering: repeat the reactor's tones as numbers ("3 1 4 2"). The
  Reactor Core, south of Engineering, pays more.
- Pilots, at the Dock: fly the Dove, the cargo shuttle, on a run to the Moon. It takes
  about a minute.
- Traders, anywhere: a market report, which trade good is cheap or dear and at which
  market.
- Scientists, in the Science Lab: find the next number in a row of readings ("18").
- Security officers, in the Cargo Bay: spot the smuggler among the travellers ("2").

After a shift you rest for 2 minutes (5 after a cargo run). A stuffed pancake from the
Cantina's bar halves your next break, and an iced coffee gives half again as much XP for ten
minutes.

### Missions

There's a board of three missions each day: bring things from one place to another.

1. "missions" reads the board.
2. "accept 1" takes mission 1.
3. Go where the things are and type "take".
4. Carry them to where they belong and type "complete".

"abandon mission" drops the one you're on.

### Levels, rank and the daily bonus

Work and missions give XP. Each level raises your pay a little, gives you a rank ("senior
pilot") and, at some levels, a keycard. "rank" says your level and how far the next one
is.

"daily" gives a bonus once a day: 40 credits, then 15 more for each day in a row, up to
130 on the seventh day, which also brings 3 strawberry seeds. Missing a day starts again
at 40.

## Money and shops

Credits are only for playing: they have no real-money value, and can't be bought or
cashed out.

### The markets

The station has four markets, each trading its own goods:

- the Spice Market, west of Hydroponics: coffee, spices and crops;
- the Ice Depot, north of the Cargo Bay: comet ice, helium-3 and frost pearls;
- the Mineral Exchange, north of the Dock: ores and meteorites;
- the parts counter in the Workshop, east of Engineering: salvage and memory chips.

The signpost on the Promenade ("look at the signpost") lists them, with what each trades
and the way there.

- At a market, "prices" (or "list") says what that market buys and sells, at its own
  prices; "prices crops" or "prices coffee" only those.
- "buy 2 coffee", "sell 5 iron", "sell all ore"; "sell all" sells everything this market
  buys.
- Anywhere else, "prices coffee" tells you the nearest market for coffee and the way:
  "Nearest market for coffee: the Spice Market, north, then 2 west." "prices" alone names
  the markets near you. Buying or selling away from the right market says the same.
- In a shop, "prices" is the shop's own list; at Second Orbit, what it would pay you.

Buying costs a fee and selling loses a little more, less for traders, so buying back
what you just sold always loses. Every sale also moves the price down a little. Other
worlds have their own markets, and "prices" works the same way there.

### Farming

You have your own plots in Hydroponics: 2 to start, up to 8.

- "plots" shows them.
- Buy seeds at Star Supply or at the seed rack in Hydroponics ("list" shows them), for
  example "buy tomato seeds".
- "plant tomato", then "water" once while it grows for one more crop.
- "harvest" when it's ripe. You must be in Hydroponics to harvest.

Crops grow in real time: water spinach in 10 minutes, chilli in 30, tomatoes in an hour,
strawberries in 2, vanilla in 4 (from level 3), dragon fruit in 8 (level 5) and moon
melons in a day (level 8). Orbit tells you when something is ripe. "buy plot" adds a plot.

### Mining and salvage

At the Asteroid Belt, "mine" gives a lump of ore every 25 seconds or so; a better drill
from Gearworks makes it quicker. The Belt Platform gives mostly iron and nickel. Out on
the Asteroid Surface (an EVA suit) and in the Crystal Cave (a suit and a headlamp) you
find rarer, dearer ore. The old miner on the platform buys ore, for a little less than
the Mineral Exchange pays.

Outside the hull, in the Debris Field, "collect" salvages scrap and sometimes something
valuable. Ore and salvage go in your bag, which holds 20 goods (a big bag from Star
Supply holds 30).

### Shops

- Star Supply, on the East Promenade: the basics (a pocket mapper, a headlamp, a
  beacon, a big bag, seeds, snacks and more farm plots).
- The Cantina's bar: iced coffee, stuffed pancakes and prawn crackers.
- The Mall Ring, up the lift from the Upper Lift Lobby:
  Gearworks (devices such as the holo mapper, the scanner, the communicator, keycards
  and the EVA suit; better drills; job tools that raise your pay), Orbit Outfitters
  (clothes and titles), Cosy Corner (furniture for your cabin), Whiskers & Widgets
  (pets), Starglint Jewellers (rings, and weddings), the Food Court, the Orbit Shipyard
  (ships), and Second Orbit.
- Second Orbit buys your things back for part of their price: "list" there says what it
  would pay, "sell headlamp" sells it.

Prices move a little each day, and each shop has one special of the day, cheaper still.

### Using and wearing things

- "list" shows what the shop you're in sells; "buy mapper" buys.
- "look at mapper" says what a thing does, and "x mapper" what you can do with it.
- "use" and a thing uses it: drink, eat, place a beacon.
- "wear" and a thing puts it on (a headlamp, an EVA suit, clothes, a title); "remove"
  and the thing takes it off.

### Trading and giving

To trade with another player, wherever they are while they're online: "offer Sam 3 iron
for 200 credits". Sam types "accept" or "decline" within two minutes, and both sides
change hands at once, or nothing does. "cancel offer" takes your offer back. Goods,
mission cargo, seeds, food, furniture and clothes can be traded; devices, keycards and
titles can't.

To give something to someone in the same room: "give Maya 50 credits", "give Maya 2
coffee".

## Ships and other worlds

### The worlds

"worlds" lists the worlds of the simulation, how far each is and the fares:

- the Moon (Moon Base Tranquility, with its helium tunnels);
- Karmina, the red planet (farming domes and rust flats);
- Glasir, the ice moon (an ice quarry);
- the Drift Bazaar, a market in a hollow asteroid, with no customs;
- Evergrove, a fantasy world of woods, a village and a castle;
- Lumina City, neon streets, a night market and courier gigs;
- Pixel Pier, the arcade world.

Each world has its own small compass map, sounds and prices. The Gate, the ferry and
your own ship all arrive at a world's port.

### The Gate and the ferry

- The Gate, in the Gate Hall east of the Upper Lift Lobby: "gate to Karmina" takes you
  there at once, for a fee (45 credits to the Moon, more the further you go).
- The ferry, from the Dock: "ferry to Karmina" is slow but cheap. It leaves every two
  minutes.

On other worlds you find both at the port.

### Your own ship

Buy a ship at the Orbit Shipyard on the Mall Ring: the Swiftlet shuttle (2,500 credits,
level 2), the Heron courier (9,000, level 5), the Buffalo hauler (16,000, level 7) or the
Hornbill explorer (40,000, level 12). You own one at a time; a new one takes the old in
for half its price. It docks in the Hangar, west of the Dock.

- "embark" to go aboard, and "disembark" to step off.
- "fly to Karmina" from aboard. Pilots fly a quarter faster.
- "refuel" at any port; each world has its own fuel price.
- "load 20 ice" moves goods from your bag to the hold; "unload" moves them back.
- "cargo" tells you the hold, the fuel and where the ship is.
- "name ship" and a name gives it one.
- "invite Sam" while aboard lets a friend come along: Sam types "embark" and your name.

### Trade runs and customs

Every world buys and sells at its own prices, so buying cheap on one world and selling
dear on another is a trade run. With your ship docked on the same world, what doesn't fit
in your bag goes into its hold. Aboard a Hornbill, its scanner bay reads another world's
market prices from afar: "prices Glasir".

Some worlds' customs search travellers for contraband and fine them. The Gate is searched
most often, the ferry less, a ship least.

### Things to do on other worlds

- The Moon: "mine" helium-3 in the Helium Tunnels.
- Karmina: "collect" in the Farming Domes, and on the Rust Flats with an EVA suit.
- Glasir: "collect" in the Ice Quarry (a suit), and in the Blue Crevasse (a suit and a
  light).
- Evergrove: in the woods, "face moss sprite" is a test of wits with a creature. Nobody
  gets hurt; a win leaves you something rare and some XP.
- Lumina City: "gig" at the Courier Hub gives you a parcel to carry, walking by compass,
  to another room before time runs out.

## Events

### What happens

Now and then something happens to everyone, announced with a sound of its own:

- a meteor shower: "collect" meteorites at the Observation Deck, on the Hull Walkway or
  in the Moon's Sea of Dust;
- a cargo spill: "collect" crates in the Cargo Bay;
- a runaway robot pet: "listen" says which way its beeps come from, and "catch" in the
  right room catches it;
- a stowaway: "search" where you are, and it says which way to look next;
- a comet: "watch" it from the Observation Deck;
- a runaway maintenance drone at the Dock that everyone fixes together: "fix drone". The
  more you help, the bigger your reward;
- a dust storm on Karmina: "join" in the Storm Shelter;
- and a solar storm, a market boom or crash on one world, a double XP hour, happy hour at
  the Cantina and a power cut.

"events" says what's on now and what's coming. "join" takes part where an event asks for
it.

### Weekly and yearly events

Every week there's a trading fair on the Mall Ring, a jackpot night at the Casino Corner,
a night rush in Lumina City (courier gigs pay double) and the duel tournament on Pixel
Pier. Every year there's the station's birthday on 25 September ("open gift"), New Year
fireworks to "watch", and the Lantern Festival.

At the Lantern Festival, lanterns in the Star Dome Hall are free, and every lantern lit
counts towards a goal: reaching it brings everyone a gift. On any other day, "light
lantern" there lights one for 3 credits, and "ring bell" rings the star bell.

### Parties

In your own cabin, "host party" opens it to everyone for half an hour. Guests type "visit"
and your name from the Crew Quarters. You can host one every two hours.

### Event reminders

"remind me" sets an ordinary Hariku reminder five minutes before the next event on the
list; "remind me" and a name, such as "remind me tournament", for that one. The "Remind
me of the next event" button in the window does the same. The reminder rings even with
Orbit closed.

## The hunt

The simulation's founder hid a prize behind a chain of hard riddles, the Lost Chord. The
first player to solve them all wins it, and later finishers win smaller prizes. It runs
in seasons; "hunt" tells you when none is running.

- "hunt" tells you your riddle, and any hints the admins have released.
- Clues are hidden in rooms around the worlds. When a clue for your riddle is where you
  are, looking there says something invites a closer look, and "investigate" finds it.
- Some clues show only with the right thing in your bag or on your head, some only at
  certain hours of the station's clock (UTC), and some are tones to listen to, like the
  reactor's. A dark room hides its clue without a light.
- "solve" and your answer answers your riddle. Capitals, accents, spaces and punctuation
  don't matter. A wrong answer makes you wait before the next try: a minute, then 2, 4,
  8 and so on, up to a day.
- A right answer gives you the next riddle, and the station hears how far you've come.
- "hunt board" shows who has come furthest, and how close the rival company, Meridian
  Grey, is.

## The arcade

The arcade is on Pixel Pier. "arcade" (anywhere) lists the games, your tokens and your
tickets.

1. On Cabinet Row, west of the Grand Arcade Hall, buy tokens at the token machine: "buy
   10 tokens" (5 credits each, up to 100 at a time).
2. "play" and a game's name there spends one token.
3. "stop game" ends a game early.

The four games are played by ear, with numbers and Enter:

- Quick Draw ("play quick draw"): five rounds. After "ready", wait for the beep, then
  type any number and Enter at once. Too soon scores nothing.
- Star Beat ("play star beat"): listen to a rhythm of short and long gaps, then tap it
  back, a number and Enter for each beat. Only the gaps between your taps count.
- Echo ("play echo"): repeat the tones as numbers, 1 to 4 from low to high; one more
  tone each time.
- Meteor Dodge ("play meteor dodge"): hear which side a meteor comes from and step away,
  4 to the left or 6 to the right (or the words left and right); from straight ahead,
  either way. It gets a little quicker each time, and three hits end it.

Good games print prize tickets. The Prize Counter, east of the Grand Arcade Hall, trades
them for prizes ("list" there): a plush comet (40 tickets), glow stars (100), a mini
arcade cabinet (300), the title Arcade Ace (500) and an enormous robot (2,500). Tickets
and prizes can't be sold or traded.

"arcade scores" reads the high score tables ("arcade scores echo" one game), and so does
the High Score Wall. A new best on a table is announced to everyone and pays 25 tickets
more.

## The casino

The Casino Corner is west of the Cantina; "casino" there lists the games.

- "dice 50 high" (8 to 12), "dice 50 low" (2 to 6) or "dice 50 seven".
- "slots 20": three reels, heard stopping from left to right.
- "blackjack 50", then "hit" or "stand". The dealer stands on 17.
- "challenge Sam 50": a coin flip with another player in the room, who accepts or
  declines. Stakes are 10 to 1,000 credits.
- "lottery" tells you about the weekly draw, on Sunday at 12:00 UTC; "buy ticket" buys a
  ticket for 10 credits.

Bets on the other games are 5 to 500 credits, and you can bet at most 5,000 an hour in
all. The house always keeps a small edge. The casino only takes and pays game credits,
which have no real-money value.

## Crews and duels

### Crews

- "crew create" and a name founds a crew (500 credits, from level 3), and you're its
  captain. A crew holds 12.
- "crew invite Sam" asks someone in; they answer "accept" or "decline".
- "crew say" and your words reach every member online, wherever they are. Crew chat is
  read with whispers (the same Read aloud setting).
- "crew" shows your crew; "crew leave" leaves it.
- As captain: "crew kick Sam", "crew captain Sam" (hand the captaincy over) and
  "crew motto" and a line.

Every XP a member earns is a point for the crew; "crews" shows the board. North of the
Hangar, the Crew Hangar is your crew's own room: only members get in, and its board shows
your crew's points and place.

### Duels

In the Zero-G Gym or on Pixel Pier's Tournament Stage, challenge someone there to a quick
draw.

1. "duel Sam", or "duel Sam 20" for a stake of up to 100 credits each.
2. Sam answers "accept" or "decline".
3. Each round: "ready", then "Draw!" a few seconds later. Type any number and Enter: the
   first one wins the round. Typing before "Draw!" loses the round.

Best of three. The winner takes both stakes, less a small fee. Walking out or leaving the
game loses the duel. "duels" shows your record; "duels off" refuses every challenge, and
"duels on" takes them again. "leaderboard duels" shows who has won the most.

### The duel tournament

Every Sunday at 15:00 UTC, the duel tournament runs for two hours on the Tournament
Stage. The players with the most duels won there take 500, 200 and 100 credits, and the
champion the title Tournament Champion. Ask Dario, the stage's host, who leads.

## Residents

The simulation has residents who aren't players, each with a voice and a story. Rocco
keeps the Cantina's bar, Oskar works in Engineering, Amara keeps the Star Dome Hall,
Captain Mateo flies the ferry from the Dock, and Dario hosts the Tournament Stage on Pixel
Pier. The shopkeepers mind their shops: Felix at Gearworks, Priya at Whiskers & Widgets
and Celeste at Starglint Jewellers. Soren comes to the Observation Deck only at night,
Shade sells things without labels in the Drift Bazaar's Back Alley, and Granny Fern lives
in Evergrove's Mossbrook Village. Poppy, Hana and Gino walk the station on their daily
rounds.

They're never counted as players: "who" doesn't list them, and "look" names them apart,
under "Residents here".

- "residents" says where each one is now.
- "talk to Rocco" greets him and says what you can ask about.
- "ask Rocco about gossip" asks. Some answers are live: the gossip, the market, events,
  who's online, the time, your own progress.
- "greet Rocco", or a gesture such as "wave to Rocco", says hello.
- "give Rocco 3 chillies" gives a gift.
- "ask Oskar about work" asks for a favour; bring what he needs, then give it to him.

They remember you. Talking each day, asking about new things, a small gift they like
(one a day counts) and their favours make them fonder of you. That opens new topics and
special errands, and in their own shop, a small discount.

## Pets

Whiskers & Widgets on the Mall Ring has a little robot, an orange space cat, a robot cat,
a mini drone, a space fox and a glow jellyfish (600 to 1,600 credits), with pet food and
treats. The rarer ones sometimes choose a player out on the worlds. A new pet has a name
already (the orange cat is Marmalade, for example) until you give it yours.

- "pet status" says how your pet is.
- "feed Kiki" (with pet food or a treat), "play with Kiki", "rest Kiki", "pat".
- "name pet Kiki", and "rename Kiki to Momo".

A pet gets hungry, bored and tired slowly, in real time. Left alone it grows sad and
quiet, but it never comes to harm, and a little care always cheers it up. Looking after
it helps it grow, from little to young to grown.

A young pet can learn tricks: "teach trick sit" a few times, when it's happy and rested,
then "trick sit" shows it off. Your pet follows you, joins in your gestures and has a
sound of its own.

## Family

### Partners

- "partner with Sam" asks Sam to be your partner; Sam answers "accept" or "decline".
- "partner" shows your partnership.
- To end it: "end partnership", then "confirm end" within a minute. Your partner is told
  kindly.

### Adopting and caring for a child

At the family desk in the Medbay, "adopt" gives a baby a home (300 credits, from
level 3). Partners decide together: the other is asked and must say yes. You may also
adopt on your own.

A child needs:

- food: baby porridge from the Food Court, then "feed Lily" (before the naming rite,
  "feed the baby");
- play: "play with Lily";
- rest: "rest Lily";
- stories: "read a story to Lily".

With care, a child grows over real days, from baby to toddler to child, and says more as
it grows. A child left alone gets quiet and asks for you, but is never harmed.

- "bring Lily" takes your child along.
- A happy child at your side helps a little with work (a bit more XP).
- Once a day, "ask Lily for help" brings you something small.
- "family" shows your family.

### The naming rite

Give your baby its name in the Star Dome Hall, with Amara there: "naming rite Lily".

## Weddings

### Proposing

Buy a ring at Starglint Jewellers on the Mall Ring (500 to 4,000 credits), then "propose
to Sam" in the same room. A yes makes you engaged; after a no, the ring stays yours.

### Booking the wedding

At the jeweller's wedding desk, book the day with four things:

- a hall: the Star Dome Hall, the Jasmine Pavilion, or the castle's Great Hall in
  Evergrove;
- a tier: simple (1,000 credits, 10 guests), grand (4,000, 30 guests and music) or
  luxurious (10,000, 60 guests and fireworks for the whole station);
- a ceremony: "starlight" (the Way of Starlight's rite) or "neutral";
- a time, in station time (UTC), from 10 minutes to 14 days ahead.

For example: "book wedding pavilion grand neutral 14:00". "tomorrow 14:00" or
"2026-10-03 14:00" work for other days.

"wedding" shows yours, and "wedding schedule" lists the weddings to come. "cancel wedding"
gives all the money back a day or more ahead, and half of it an hour or more ahead.

### Guests

"invite Sam to the wedding" sends an invitation. Guests answer "rsvp yes" or "rsvp no";
"invitations" lists theirs. At the ceremony, guests can "throw flowers", "cheer" and
"clap".

### The ceremony

Be at the hall on time, both of you: the ceremony waits 20 minutes. Do each step when
you're asked.

- The Starlight rite: each of you lights a lantern ("light lantern"), the two lights are
  joined into one ("join the lights"), a moment of silence, you each say your own vows
  ("vow" and your words), and the star bell rings three times.
- The neutral ceremony: your vows ("vow" and your words), each partner's "yes", and your
  signatures ("sign").

### Afterwards

The station hears the news, and you get a title and a keepsake. "read memory" brings the
day back; the guests who came can read it too. Your vows are kept in that memory.

## Achievements and leaderboards

Reaching a mark (rooms walked, a level, a first harvest, ore mined, a week of daily
bonuses and more) earns an achievement, some credits and sometimes a title. The big ones
are news for the whole station.

- "achievements" lists yours and the closest ones; "achievements Sam" lists Sam's.
- "leaderboard" shows the leaders of each board.
- "leaderboard richest" shows one board and your place. The boards are richest, level,
  miners, farmers, streak, casino and duels.

## Playing from Aruna

Put "orbit" in front of any game command and tell it to Aruna, typed or spoken (with
Voice Control, the wake phrase works too):

- "orbit north", "orbit say hello", "orbit whisper Maya see you later"
- "orbit who is online", "orbit daily", "orbit harvest", "orbit profile", "orbit x here"
- "orbit status", "orbit voices off", "orbit quit"

The answer is spoken, a whole reply at once, and shows in Aruna's Last result. If you
aren't connected, Orbit connects first, which takes a moment. Harvesting still needs your
character in Hydroponics, and work needs your workplace: Aruna only sends the command.

Open it with the window's Settings button, or type "settings" in the game.

  Connect and Status: see Getting started.
- Read with: who reads the game (see Reading and listening).
- Speak messages: read the game aloud at all. Off, you read the Messages box yourself;
  Orbit still says when it connects or leaves.
- A different voice for each player (Hariku Voice).
- Speak my own lines in my character voice.

- Read aloud what others do and the station's news: Others' chat and gestures; Whispers
  and crew chat to me; Others' shouts; Others' arrivals and departures; Credits and
  things others give me; Station announcements and news; Special events' news (meteor
  showers, parties, fireworks). These only narrow down what other players and the
  station do: a reply to your own command (you buy, sell, get, give, are paid, win or

- While the Orbit window is closed, read: Everything; Only whispers, my name and station
  news (the default); or Nothing. Sounds follow the same choice.
- When I close the Orbit window, and Log me out when the window is closed and I've been
  idle for: see Leaving or hiding the window.
- Connect when Hariku starts.
- Ambience while the Orbit window is open, and Ambience volume.
- Sounds, and Effects volume.
- Other players' sounds.

### Settings you can type

In the game, or from Aruna with "orbit" in front:

- "reader mixed", "reader nvda", "reader voices"
- "speech off" or "speech on": reading messages aloud
- "voices off" or "voices on": players' own voices
- "my lines off" or "my lines on": your own words in your voice
- "names off" or "names on": a player's name before their words
- "ambience off" or "ambience on", "ambience volume 30"
- "sounds off" or "sounds on", "effects volume 60"

### Ignoring a player

"ignore Sam" hides Sam's words, gestures and offers on your computer: they are neither
shown nor read. "unignore Sam" hears them again, and "ignored" lists who you ignore. The
list is also in Settings, Ignored players.

## Leaving or hiding the window

### Closing the window

Escape or the close button hides the window, and you stay connected in the background.
Orbit tells you how to open it again. While it's closed, only what you chose in "While
the Orbit window is closed, read" is heard, and the ambience stops.

If you'd rather leave whenever you close the window, choose Leave Orbit in "When I close
the Orbit window".

### Leaving Orbit

"quit", the Leave Orbit button, "orbit quit" from Aruna and quitting Hariku all log you
out at once, and the others hear that you left.

### Away and logged out

With the window closed and nothing typed for five minutes, others see you as away. After
30 minutes, Orbit logs you out. Choose 15 or 60 minutes, or Never, in "Log me out when
the window is closed and I've been idle for".

### When the connection drops

Orbit says it's offline and keeps trying, and says "Reconnected." when it's back. If it
comes back within a minute, you're still where you were and nobody noticed. If you
connect with the same character from somewhere else, the older connection closes.

## Moving your character to another computer

Your character lives on the computer that made it. To play it on another computer, move
it with a transfer code.

1. On the old computer, while connected, press "Move my character to another computer"
   in Preferences, Orbit, or type "transfer code".
2. Orbit shows the code in "Transfer code" and reads it: 16 letters in four groups of
   four, like ABCD-EFGH-JKLM-NPQR.
3. On the other computer, in Preferences, Orbit, type it in "Have a transfer code? Type it
   here" and press "Use the code", within 10 minutes. The code works once.

The character is moved, not copied: the first computer can't play it any more. If you
lose your computer, an admin can make a code for you.

## Privacy

Orbit connects to its game server only when you open it, press Connect or give it a
command, or when Hariku starts if you turned that on, always over an encrypted
connection.

- It sends your character's name and job the first time, a secret made on your computer
  (it stands for a password) and what you type or say in the game. The game is in
  English whatever Hariku's language is, so your language isn't needed.
- The server keeps your character: its name, job, description, credits, things,
  progress, what the residents remember of it, and its pets, family and weddings,
  including the vows you write. It keeps only a hash of your secret, and no password or
  email.
- Chat is passed on to whoever hears it and is not stored.
- "remind me" makes an ordinary Hariku reminder on your computer; the server isn't told.
- Your settings and the players you ignore stay on your computer.
- If you use Edge Voices, what Hariku Voice reads is sent to Microsoft to be spoken.

Hariku's privacy notice has the details.
